Head to head

Neuro Sampler Suite vs Akai MPC Key 37

The MPC Key 37 is a standalone production keyboard: 37 synth-action keys with aftertouch, 16 velocity-sensitive pads, a touchscreen, 32 GB of storage and a $899 launch price. NSS is software that runs on a Mac with a controller such as the MPK mini mk2. The scores are 0–10, judged on the evidence in the table.

Why it sits a level above its class

Five things few instruments in its class do

Its class is MPC-style slice samplers you can play live. Most of them treat a sample as audio to cut. NSS treats it as music it can understand.

Where NSS is ahead

  • Understanding the audio. Stems, drum separation, per-slice pitch, key and chord names. The MPC workflow gives you a sample. NSS gives you a sample it can describe.
  • Harmony. Timbre chords, chord pads in the song key, next-chord suggestions and four melodic arp modes.
  • Scale. 128 pads, 12 class outputs, a 51k-file library indexed in place, 234 learnable controls.
  • Openness. Free, AGPL source, about 340 automated checks, and it runs in any DAW that hosts VST3 or AU. It's also designed to run inside MPC 3 software, though that is untested on MPC gear so far.

Where the MPC Key 37 is ahead

  • It's a finished instrument. It works with no computer, and it has a real 37-key keybed with aftertouch and wheels.
  • Touchscreen and hands-on flow. It was designed as one object, where NSS is software plus a controller.
  • Built-in sounds. Synth engines and content ship in the box. NSS plays your own library.
  • Proven on stage. NSS is in beta, and its hands-on sessions in Live, Logic and MPC 3 are still open.

The verdict

On intelligence, NSS is superior: its sampler can split a song, sort the drums, name the chords and re-voice them, and the MPC Key 37 doesn't do all of that. On physical instrument design, the MPC Key 37 wins. They don't have to compete. NSS follows MIDI clock and is designed to run inside MPC 3 software, so the MPC can be the body and NSS the brain.
